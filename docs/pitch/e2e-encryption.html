Encrypting the journal

You asked for end-to-end encryption because the data "could be sensitive", and steered: encrypt the names and quantities, dates aren't a big deal. This spike builds that (option O1) on the mockup/e2e-encryption branch against the Firestore emulator only, and shows what it still leaves visible, so you can judge it against hiding timing too (O3). Every claim is tagged SOURCED with its source or INFERENCE.

Recommendation: O1, content sealed before 1.0, dates in plaintext

It fits your steer, keeps every query, index and ownership rule working, and is now working code: crypto core, rules with 41 new tests (the 72 existing pass unchanged), sealed reads and writes, the first-launch migrator and the key screens. The cost is what the server still sees: who logs, when, how often per opaque vice, and how many vices. The console view below shows exactly that. Hiding timing needs O3, about twice the work, against your steer.

Sub-calls I recommend with it: recovery key offered on Today after a week (variant A); enc stored as a Blob; every vice padded to 285 bytes; a 32-character recovery key that locates a typo; owner may delete keys/{uid} for "Start a new journal". Each is below with its alternatives.

The four options

As pitched before the spike. O1 is the one built here; O3 is judged from the console view, not built.

O0No E2EE, accurate wording

Say what is true: Firestore encrypts at rest with Google-held keys SOURCED; insights stay on the device. You, Google and anyone who breaches the project can read every entry.

S. Goes against the aim you stated.

O1Seal content before 1.0 Recommended

Names, units, quantities and every later note or trigger go in one sealed enc field. ownerID, viceID, createdAt, archived stay plaintext. Key in iCloud Keychain, plus a recovery key.

L. Built in this spike.

O2Same, deferred to 1.1

Ship 1.0 in plaintext, migrate strangers' production data later. 1.0 parsers must already read both shapes, and the most revealing fields get written in plaintext first.

L+, across two releases. Riskier than O1.

O3Sealed vault, timing hidden too

Per-user encrypted op log in fixed-size segments, flushed at random times with cover traffic. The only design that hides when, since Firestore stamps its own createTime SOURCED.

XL, about 2x O1 INFERENCE. Against your steer on dates.

Full costs and risks of each option (from the original pitch)

O0. A breach means notifying users under GDPR Art. 34, because the 34(3)(a) encryption exemption is unavailable SOURCED. Years of alcohol or drug logs next to Apple Health correlations are likely Art. 9 health data INFERENCE. CMEK is no middle way: new databases only, and the project owner still reads plaintext SOURCED.

O1. C12 (quantity ≥ 1), C13 (name 1–60) and the unit enum stop being server checks and live only in lib/data/limits.dart; a modified client can corrupt only its own data INFERENCE. The claim narrows to "we can see when you log, not what", as Apple's Advanced Data Protection does for its metadata SOURCED. You lose console readability of your own data. Top risks: a second key minted beside ciphertext (data loss); every device and the recovery key lost; a bundle-ID rename orphaning keychain items SOURCED; the app reaching a device before rules R1 are live, which denies every write; crash logs or notifications carrying decrypted names.

O2. 1.0 plaintext stays in Google's deletion pipeline for about 180 days SOURCED and in the PITR window SOURCED. Users who never update keep writing plaintext, so the strict rules (R2) can't land. Today's parsers cast unconditionally (json['name'] as String), so a pre-encryption build loses the whole list on the first sealed document SOURCED: lib/data/vice.dart on master.

O3. Retires the per-entry documents: every query becomes an in-memory filter, all composite indexes go, and it replaces the documented data shape, which needs your explicit yes under the hard rule. A device lost before a flush loses up to 6 hours of entries. Auth token refresh still shows when the app is open SOURCED. The sync engine is homemade and its parameters are guesses INFERENCE.

Sub-decisions inside O1

Each built or tested in the spike. One recommendation each; none is decided.

1. When the recovery key is first offered

A. Card on Today after a week Recommended

Asks when there is a journal worth protecting. Sign-up stays fast.

B. Step at sign-up, with Later

Everyone sees it once, before they have anything to lose.

Why A: the key syncs silently through iCloud Keychain, so the recovery key matters only when every device and the keychain are lost INFERENCE. Onboarding aims at a first log in under a minute SOURCED: ideas/v1-must-haves/first-run-onboarding.md, and Tuta's study found users skipping recovery codes shown at setup SOURCED. The exposure is a week of entries on a phone lost with iCloud Keychain off INFERENCE. Settings has the key from day one either way.

2. enc as a Blob or a base64 string

Blob Recommended

Rules check exact byte sizes (93, 285, 1053, 4125). 25% smaller.

base64 string

Lengths 124, 380, 1404, 5500. Uses only types the rules-language page documents.

Why Blob: the emulator probe passes: a Blob is bytes, size() counts bytes, and it is neither a string nor accepted where a string is required SOURCED: tests/firestore-rules/bytes-probe.test.js. But the rules-language page does not list bytes for is SOURCED, only the rules.Bytes reference does SOURCED. Production could differ from the emulator; a one-document check in the beta project (r2-testflight) settles it before any app build ships. Falling back to strings is a one-line change in the rules and in journal_reader.dart INFERENCE.

3. Padding for vices

Every vice at least 256 Recommended

All 285 bytes, from "A" to a 60-character name, 30 emoji or 60 escaped quotes.

Smallest bucket that fits

Short names 93 bytes, long ones 285: name length leaks.

Why 256: 192 bytes more per vice, a handful of documents per user. Only a name of 60 control characters reaches 1053 SOURCED: test/crypto/journal_cipher_test.dart.

4. Recovery key format

32 characters, 32-bit checksum Recommended

6EQC-8QDN-D27K-TC5R-ZZJ3-6WTD-3864-AX35. Locates one wrong character.

27 characters, Crockford mod-37 check

Five fewer to type; detects a typo but can't say where.

Why 32: the hardest moment is typing it on a new device with no other way in. Locating the error turns "doesn't match" into "character 2 of group 4". Search takes 71 ms on this Mac in Dart SOURCED: test/bench/e2ee_bench_test.dart.

5. Who may delete keys/{uid}

The owner Recommended

Start a new journal and account deletion work. A buggy client could delete it alone and mint a second key.

Owner, plus a keyId guard on every sealed write

Rules get() the keys doc and match a key ID in each blob. Closes the hole server-side.

Why owner only, for now: the guard costs a billed read on every write, even denied ones SOURCED, and batched writes allow 20 such calls, so migration batches would shrink from 200 to about 19 documents same page. The client invariant (never mint while keys/{uid} exists on the server) is tested against a fake SOURCED: test/crypto/key_service_test.dart. Revisit if anything else ever deletes that document.

If you pick O1

The order matters, because a build that seals before R1 is in production gets every write denied.

  1. Enable rules deployment (DEPLOY_FIRESTORE_RULES), so #33 and then R1 reach production through CI SOURCED: HANDOVER.md.
  2. Land the prerequisites already queued: parsers that default missing fields, the shared write layer, the bundle-ID lock, and the macOS target.
  3. Run the iOS end-to-end pass this spike couldn't: sign up, log, wipe the simulator keychain, unlock with the recovery key, legacy docs converted, and time 2,000 entries.
  4. Ship the app build that reads both shapes and migrates on first launch; then strict rules (R2) once your devices have migrated.

The 43 new rules tests
e2ee R1: vices
  - allows a sealed create (add_vice.dart: enc, ownerID, createdAt, archived)
  - still allows the plaintext create, for builds that predate encryption
  - allows enc of exactly n bytes and denies n-1 and n+1, for n = 93, 285, 1053, 4125
  - denies enc as a base64 string of a valid blob
  - denies a mixed shape: enc beside name or unit
  - denies a sealed vice owned by someone else, or with a missing field
  - allows the migration update: plaintext out, enc in, in one write
  - allows migrating a legacy vice and adding its missing archived
  - denies a migration that leaves plaintext behind
  - denies a migration with a bad enc length, or by another user
  - denies a downgrade: enc out, plaintext back
  - denies a second migration that writes plaintext back beside enc
  - allows re-sealing a sealed vice (a rename), which a repeat migration also looks like
  - allows archiving a sealed vice without touching enc
  - allows the vice list queries over sealed and plaintext vices together

e2ee R1: activities
  - allows a sealed create against an owned vice (record dialog: doc().set())
  - allows enc of exactly n bytes and denies n-1 and n+1, for n = 93, 285, 1053, 4125
  - denies a mixed shape: enc beside quantity
  - C14 still holds: denies a sealed activity on another user
  - denies a sealed activity owned by someone else, or from a guest
  - allows the migration update, including a zero-quantity and an orphaned legacy activity
  - allows a migration batch of 200 updates (the migrator pages by 200)
  - denies a migration that keeps quantity
  - denies a downgrade, and quantity written back beside enc
  - allows the editor re-sealing and moving a sealed activity in time
  - denies moving a sealed activity to another vice, or reassigning it
  - allows the day-window query over sealed activities: createdAt stays plaintext

e2ee R1: keys/{uid}
  - allows the owner to create their keys doc
  - denies creating keys for another uid, or as a guest
  - denies the wrong shape: sizes, types, extra or missing fields
  - is create-only: a second key over the first is denied
  - keeps keyId and check immutable
  - allows re-wrapping under a new recovery key
  - allows the owner to read it and denies a stranger or a guest
  - allows the owner to delete it (Start a new journal) and denies a stranger

probe: is bytes on a Blob
  - accepts a 93-byte Blob as bytes and measures it in bytes
  - does not treat a Blob as a string, or a string as bytes
