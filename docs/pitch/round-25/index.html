Call 2

May the watch hold your account's ID?

Today
As built, the watch app stores your account's ID (a random-looking string that Firebase gave the account) and sends it back with each log.
Why it is there
So a log tapped under one account is never saved to another, and a log that arrives while the iPhone is signed out waits for its own account. The iPhone's widgets already work this way.
What it is not
It is not a password or a key. Nobody can sign in or read the journal with it. The journal key and the sign-in never leave the iPhone.
Why asked
I had planned for the watch to hold nothing about the account. This is a departure, so it is yours to accept.
Waiting on this
Nothing. If you do nothing, A ships with the watch app.
A · recommended

Keep the account ID

  • No more work.
  • Cost: an identifier, useless by itself, sits in the watch app's storage.
B

Use a throwaway token

  • The watch holds a random token per sign-in; the iPhone maps it back.
  • About half a day.
  • Risk: a new table on the iPhone that must outlive sign-out, or late logs are lost.
Call 3

Should Siri speak the answer to a journal question?

Today
Build 1870, on your iPhone now: say "Ask Penchant", Siri asks "What would you like to ask your journal?", then Penchant opens on Ask with the question already asked. It needs Settings' "Ask your journal" switch on; with it off, Siri opens Settings and sends nothing. Untested on a phone until you try it.
One breath is out
"Ask Penchant how many coffees this week" in a single sentence is not possible. Apple's tools refuse free text inside a Siri phrase; I tried and the build fails.
The option
Siri says "11 coffees since Monday" without opening the app. It works only when the answer is a single number; anything with a chart or table still opens the app.
Why asked
You picked "ask without opening the app". It is possible on paper but has two risks only you can accept.
Waiting on this
Nothing. If you do nothing, Siri keeps opening the app.
