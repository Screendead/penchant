Three more things Claude could do, and what each sends off the phone

You picked three ideas: the weekly review in plain words, asking your journal questions, and one tailored follow-up question. Each is a separate decision below. All three would use the server function that the note reader already uses, sit behind their own off-by-default switch, and fall back to what the app does today when offline or when the reply fails a check.

The examples in the mock-ups are invented. Costs use Anthropic's list prices; your workspace currently allows only the smallest model (Haiku), so any option naming Sonnet needs you to raise its limit from zero.

Today the weekly review is a page of lists: each habit's total against its usual week, the busiest hours, the most common tags, and which findings are new or no longer hold. It is accurate and takes effort to read. It can also notify you on Monday at 09:00.

The change: a short paragraph at the top that says the same things in sentences. The phone still does all the arithmetic. Claude only turns the finished figures into prose, and the lists stay underneath as the source.

Leaves the phoneThe week's computed figures, with each habit called "Habit A", "Habit B". Tag names from the standard list.
Stays on the phoneHabit names, every entry, every note, your own-words tags, Health data. Names are put back after the reply.

The safeguard: the phone checks that every number in the paragraph is one it sent. If one isn't, the paragraph is dropped and you see today's lists alone.

RecommendedHaiku writes it, numbers checked

Runs once a week when you open the review. About two hundredths of a cent each time.

Risk: plain or slightly stiff prose. A check on numbers cannot catch a misleading emphasis, so the instruction forbids causes, advice and comparison with other people.

Build: about 2 days.

Sonnet writes it

Better sentences and judgement about what to lead with. About a third of a cent a week.

Needs: you raise Sonnet's limit in the workspace.

Build: about 2 days.

Sentences without a model

The phone fills fixed sentence patterns itself. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Risk: reads like a form letter, and every new kind of finding needs its own pattern written.

Build: about 3 days.

Why now: nothing forces it. Waiting on this: nothing. If you do nothing: the weekly review stays as lists.

That is a "why", which Insights answers by comparison. It has one finding about Fridays.

Open it in Insights

Today a question like "when was my last week without wine?" means paging back through days or reading a chart. Trends and Insights answer the questions they were built for and no others.

The change: a question box. Claude does not see your journal. It reads the question and turns it into a precise query (which habit, what to count, over what period, filtered by which tag, weekday or hour). The phone runs that query on your entries and shows a figure and a small chart.

Leaves the phoneThe question you typed, and your habits' names, which are encrypted everywhere else. Without the names it cannot tell which habit "wine" means.
Stays on the phoneEvery entry, amount, time, tag and note, and the answer itself.

The limit, by design: it counts and finds; it does not explain. A "why" or "does X cause Y" question is passed to Insights, which only reports a pattern when the statistics hold, and says when they don't. The model never does ad-hoc statistics.

RecommendedClaude writes the query, the phone answers

Haiku, about two hundredths of a cent a question, an expected 1 to 2 seconds, unmeasured.

Risk: a misread question gives a confident wrong answer, so each answer states the query in words ("Weeks with no wine entries, counted back from today") for you to check. Questions outside the query language get "can't answer that yet".

Build: about 6 days: the query language and its runner are most of it. I'd test Haiku against Sonnet on 40 written questions before choosing.

Claude sees the results and writes the answer

A back-and-forth: Claude asks the phone for figures, reads them, may ask for more, then answers in sentences. Handles looser questions.

Risk: your figures leave the phone, answers take 5 to 15 seconds, and the model can word a guess as a fact. Needs Sonnet.

Build: about 9 days.

Don't build it

Add the two or three most-wanted fixed answers to Trends instead, such as "longest gap" and "last week without".

Build: about 2 days, nothing sent anywhere.

Why now: nothing forces it; it is the largest of the three. Waiting on this: nothing. If you do nothing: it is not built.

Today the heavy-day prompt asks the same thing every time, "Anything different about today?", and a note is whatever you chose to write. Nothing follows up, so notes tend to describe what happened and stop short of the detail that makes them useful later.

The change: after you write a note about a day or an entry, one optional question that picks up something you wrote. Your answer is saved, encrypted, with the note. It arrives in the same reply as the tags, so it costs no extra wait and almost nothing extra.

Leaves the phoneThe note's text, which the note reader already sends when it is switched on. Nothing more.
Stays on the phoneYour answer to the question, and everything else.

The danger is tone. This is a journal about drinking, smoking and similar. A model free to write any question will, sooner or later, ask one that sounds like a judgement or a diagnosis.

RecommendedClaude picks a written question and a phrase of yours

About a dozen questions are written by us in advance ("Was that already the plan this morning?", "What happened just before?", "Who suggested it?"). Claude chooses the one that fits and the phrase from your note it refers to, as in the mock-up. The phone checks the phrase is really in the note.

Risk: less varied; after a few weeks you will recognise the set. Tone cannot go wrong, because no question is written by the model.

Build: about 2 days.

Claude writes the question

Fully tailored, under rules: one question, about circumstances only, never amounts, never advice.

Risk: rules reduce bad questions and cannot rule them out, and one bad question in a private journal costs trust that is hard to win back.

Build: about 2 days, plus a written test set of notes to review its questions against before release.
