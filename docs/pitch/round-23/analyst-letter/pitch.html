The analyst and the letter: two jobs for Claude's largest models

You asked to see two ideas that need a top model: an analyst that investigates a question about your journal, and a long review written every few months. Both send more off the phone than anything the app does today, so each call below says exactly what would go. Both depend on the rebuilt "Ask your journal", where Claude writes database queries that run on the phone; that rebuild is in progress.

The examples are invented. Costs and times are estimates from Anthropic's list prices (Opus: $4 per million tokens in, $20 out); nothing here has been run. Your workspace allows Opus zero requests a minute today, so either feature needs that raised, and your $10 a month spend limit caps both.

Test it in Insights

Today Ask answers one question with one query, and Claude never sees the result. So it can count, but it cannot notice anything, follow a lead or explain. A "why" is handed to Insights, which only tests the comparisons it was built with.

The change: a second mode, switched on separately. Claude writes a query, the phone runs it and sends back the result, Claude reads it and decides what to look at next, up to about a dozen steps, then writes a short account with charts. It takes roughly 30 to 90 seconds.

Leaves the phoneYour question, habit names, and the results of its queries: counts, totals, dates, times of day and tag names. Capped at 200 rows a query.
Stays on the phoneAll note and day-note text: in this mode the database refuses any query that reads those columns. Health, places and calendar data are never in the database at all.

Safeguards: every number in the account must be one Claude was shown, as with the weekly review. Its queries are listed for you to open. It is labelled as a reading, never a finding, and a causal claim is handed to Insights, which can refuse.

Before each investigation the app says "This sends the results of its queries to Claude" with Go and Cancel. About 10 to 20 cents each, so your $10 limit allows 50 to 100 a month.

Set up that experiment
Written by Claude · what was sent

Today nothing reads your notes as a body. Insights works on amounts, times and tags. The note reader looks at one note at a time and forgets it. What you wrote in August is never set beside what you wrote in October.

The change: on request, never automatically, the app sends a period of your journal to Claude, which writes a few hundred words: what you wrote about most, what shifted, and two or three things worth testing. Each suggestion can start a self-experiment, where the statistics judge it. The letter is saved encrypted on the phone.

Leaves the phoneFor the period chosen: every note and day note in full, and each entry's time, amount, habit name and tags. This is most of the journal.
Stays on the phoneHealth, places and calendar data, and the letter itself once written. It is not synced to your Mac in the first version.

This is the largest privacy step in the app. Anthropic's API terms say it is not used for training and not retained by default; some models carry 30-day retention, and I have not confirmed which side Opus 5.5 falls on. I would confirm that before building.

RecommendedOpus, notes included, with a preview of what is sent

Before sending, a screen shows the period, the number of notes and entries, and the text itself to scroll through, with Send and Cancel. About 50 cents to $1 a letter, a minute or two to write.

Risk: tone. A letter interprets, and an interpretation of a journal about drinking or smoking can read as a verdict. The instruction forbids advice, diagnosis and judgement, and I would have you read letters written from made-up journals before switching it on.

Build: about 5 days.
